# Ideal Customer Profile and Industries

## Primary ICP

### Technical founders and operations-heavy small businesses

Best fit:

- 5-100 person organizations,
- several SaaS tools already in use,
- manual handoffs between inboxes, spreadsheets, CRMs, support tools, accounting systems, or internal chat,
- repeated business rules that live in people's heads,
- interest in AI-assisted workflows but concern about maintainability or uncontrolled automation,
- a decision maker who can approve a fixed-scope implementation project.

Typical buyer titles:

- founder / CEO,
- COO / operations lead,
- head of customer success,
- revenue operations lead,
- technical operations lead,
- engineering manager in a smaller team.

## Secondary ICP

### Automation consultants and small agencies

Best fit:

- already build in Zapier, Make, n8n, Pipedream, or custom code,
- need better discovery, documentation, and handoff artifacts,
- inherit fragile automations,
- want a consistent way to explain AI steps, approvals, and failure paths.

SolveLang should complement their implementation stack rather than replace it.

## Strong initial use cases

- support intake and triage,
- lead qualification and routing,
- document classification and review,
- invoice intake and approval preparation,
- recurring operations reports,
- email summarization with human review,
- CRM handoff specifications,
- approval workflows.

## Industries worth testing

These are hypotheses, not proven SolveLang verticals.

### Professional services

Pain often appears in intake, proposals, client communications, approvals, and recurring reporting.

### Property services and field operations

Potential workflows include lead intake, estimate routing, job handoff, document collection, and follow-up.

### Agencies

Potential workflows include lead qualification, campaign intake, client approvals, reporting, and internal handoff.

### SaaS and software-enabled businesses

Potential workflows include support triage, onboarding, renewal-risk handoff, incident communications, and internal operations.

### Finance/administrative operations

Potential workflows include invoice preparation, document classification, routing, and human approval. Consequential financial actions should remain controlled by existing systems and human authorization.

## Poor initial fit

Do not target first:

- organizations requiring certified enterprise orchestration immediately,
- buyers whose main need is thousands of native connectors,
- safety-critical or regulated workflows requiring capabilities SolveLang has not certified,
- teams seeking a Temporal or Airflow replacement,
- buyers expecting autonomous AI workers with no human governance,
- large enterprise procurement where compliance and support requirements exceed current maturity.

## Qualification questions

A prospect becomes more attractive when several are true:

- Is the workflow repeated weekly or daily?
- Does it cross multiple people or systems?
- Are mistakes or delays expensive enough to matter?
- Is ownership unclear today?
- Can the process be described with explicit rules and review points?
- Is there a person who owns the process and can validate the workflow?
- Can the buyer start with one narrow workflow rather than a company-wide transformation?
