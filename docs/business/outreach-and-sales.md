# Outreach and Sales Playbook

## Cold email

Subject: documenting the workflow behind {{process}}

Hi {{name}},

I help teams turn fragile or undocumented business automations into readable workflow specifications before they invest in more tooling. I noticed {{specific, truthful observation}}.

If {{process}} currently involves repeated manual routing, AI-assisted decisions, or several SaaS tools, I can run a fixed-scope workflow clarity audit: map the current process, identify failure and approval points, separate deterministic rules from AI judgment, and recommend an implementation path using the tools you already have where possible.

Would a 20-minute process review be useful?

— {{sender}}

If your team has an automation that works but is hard to explain, review, or maintain, I’d be interested in mapping one process and showing where deterministic rules, AI steps, approvals, and failure paths belong. No platform replacement required.

## Discovery call structure

1. Ask the client to describe the process without naming a preferred technology.
2. Identify the trigger, owner, systems, decisions, exceptions, and failure modes.
3. Quantify volume and human effort using client data.
4. Identify AI-suitable versus deterministic steps.
5. Identify consequential actions and required human approvals.
6. Propose the smallest useful paid scope.

## Sales script

**Opening:** “I’m not trying to replace your automation stack. I’m trying to make the process readable enough that your team can review, test, and maintain it.”

**Problem:** “Where does this workflow rely on someone remembering what to do, interpreting an AI output, or fixing a brittle automation?”

**Impact:** “What happens when that step is late or wrong?”

**Current tooling:** “What already works that we should preserve?”

**Pilot:** “The smallest useful first engagement is usually a clarity audit or one workflow prototype with explicit acceptance criteria.”

## Qualification signals

Good fit:
- repeated process with a named owner
- measurable manual work or maintenance pain
- several systems or handoffs
- AI step needs governance or human review
- buyer values documentation and handoff

Poor fit:
- wants thousands of ready-made connectors
- expects guaranteed autonomous AI outcomes
- requires certified enterprise orchestration immediately
- no owner, budget, or repeatable process

## Follow-up

Send a short recap containing:
- business problem
- agreed scope
- exclusions
- client inputs required
- deliverables
- price hypothesis or quote
- next decision date
