# Industry Opportunities

These are target hypotheses, not claims of customers or traction.

## Best near-term characteristics

Prioritize organizations with repetitive knowledge work, several SaaS handoffs, visible manual routing, and a process owner who can approve rules and outcomes.

## Professional services and agencies

Potential workflows: lead intake, client onboarding, approval routing, recurring reporting, document classification, and internal handoffs.

Why it fits: agencies already combine tools and often need better documentation and client handoff.

## B2B software and SaaS teams

Potential workflows: support triage, customer-success routing, product-feedback classification, lead qualification, incident communication drafts.

Why it fits: readable logic and human-review boundaries matter when AI is inserted into customer-facing operations.

## Property and field-service operations

Potential workflows: inquiry intake, work-order classification, estimate preparation, scheduling handoff, document checks.

Boundary: avoid claiming a complete field-service platform; implementation should integrate with existing systems.

## Recruiting and staffing operations

Potential workflows: intake normalization, document classification, candidate communications drafts, approval routing, internal summaries.

Boundary: consequential employment decisions require strong human governance and should not be positioned as autonomous AI decisions.

## Finance/administrative operations

Potential workflows: invoice intake, exception routing, reconciliation preparation, approval workflows, recurring reporting.

Boundary: financial posting/payment actions should remain explicitly controlled and audited.

## Industries not to prioritize first

Avoid leading with high-regulation or high-consequence environments that require certifications, validated controls, or production guarantees SolveLang does not currently provide. The methodology may still be useful there later, but current product maturity should not be overstated.

## Selection rule

Choose a vertical only after repeated discovery reveals a common buyer, repeated workflow shape, measurable pain, and willingness to pay. Do not build an industry product from hypothetical demand alone.
